## 核心發現（Key Findings）

研究觀察到麻醉狀態下存在跨物種保守的動態特徵，表現為神經活動的內在時間尺度縮短，以及腦區間同步性降低。在獼猴中對中央中核（centromedian thalamus）進行深腦刺激，可逆轉此動態特徵並恢復行為反應性。此保守的動態表型與興奮性及抑制性神經傳導相關的保守轉錄表現相關。

## 臨床意義（Clinical Relevance）

本研究為跨物種基礎神經科學研究，顯示麻醉可能導致局部神經活動在時空上的隔離，此現象在不同物種與尺度間具有共通性；惟此為觀察與建模結果，不能直接推論為臨床麻醉機制之因果證據或療效證明。

## 研究意義（Research Relevance）

本研究建立了跨物種、跨尺度的麻醉神經動態資料庫與分析框架，有助於未來探索麻醉意識消失的共同神經生物學基礎，並為後續轉譯研究（如深腦刺激應用）提供假說基礎。

## PubMed Abstract

The behavioral effects of anesthetics are highly conserved across species, hinting at shared and fundamental underlying mechanisms. Here we compile a dataset of multiscale neural activity during wakefulness and anesthesia, encompassing human, macaque, marmoset, mouse, zebrafish and nematode. Applying massive feature extraction, we characterize local neural dynamics across >6,000 time-series features. This reveals a conserved dynamical profile of anesthesia across species, characterized by shorter intrinsic timescales of neural activity and dampened inter-regional synchrony. Deep-brain stimulation of the macaque centromedian thalamus reverses this profile and restores behavioral responsiveness. This conserved dynamical phenotype covaries with conserved transcriptional profiles of excitatory and inhibitory neurotransmission. Biophysical modeling provides a potential mechanistic link between the macroscale dynamical phenotype of anesthesia and microscale effects of key molecular targets on the timescales of synaptic excitation and inhibition. These analyses reveal a shared neural endpoint of anesthesia: across species and scales, anesthetics induce spatiotemporal isolation of local neural activity.
