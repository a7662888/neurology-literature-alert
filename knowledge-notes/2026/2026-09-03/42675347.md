觀察到QTM灌流速度在主觀認知下降階段（包含海馬迴區域）即已下降，而CBF主要在輕度認知障礙與阿茲海默症階段才出現下降。性別與診斷分組之交互作用在灰質與白質QTM指標達統計顯著，但CBF未見此交互作用。QTM（尤其在女性）與血漿NfL、GFAP呈負相關，並與MMSE分數相關；頂葉QTM部分中介海馬迴體積與MMSE表現之關聯。

## PubMed Abstract

INTRODUCTION: We tested whether quantitative transport mapping (QTM)-derived perfusion velocity would detect earlier and more sex-specific microvascular alterations than cerebral blood flow (CBF) across the Alzheimer's disease (AD) continuum.

METHODS: In 182 participants (cognitively normal = 53, subjective cognitive decline [SCD] = 48, mild cognitive impairment [MCI] = 31, AD = 50), seven-delay arterial spin labeling was used to derive CBF and QTM velocity. Age/sex-adjusted models assessed diagnostic and sex effects, associations with plasma biomarkers and Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE), and mediation of the hippocampal volume-cognition relationship.

RESULTS: QTM velocity was reduced already in SCD, including the hippocampus, whereas CBF declined mainly in MCI/AD. Sex × Diagnosis interactions were significant for QTM in gray and white matter, but not for CBF. QTM, especially in females, was inversely associated with plasma NfL/GFAP, related to MMSE, and parietal QTM partially mediated the hippocampal volume-MMSE association.

DISCUSSION: QTM is a non-invasive biomarker more sensitive than CBF to early, female-biased microvascular transport dysfunction in AD.
