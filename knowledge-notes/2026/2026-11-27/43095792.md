## 核心發現（Key Findings）

與對照組相比，帕金森氏症患者之SAI降低。在調整相關臨床共變項後，SAI與PD-CRS總分（β=-0.19, P=0.013）及後皮質功能分數（β=-0.44, P<0.001）顯著相關，但與額葉-皮質下功能分數無顯著相關（β=-0.13, P=0.103）。中介分析顯示，SAI經由後側cBF體積對整體認知存在顯著間接路徑（間接β=-0.071，95% CI -0.158至-0.013，佔總關聯的42%），納入中介變項後殘餘直接路徑不再具統計顯著性（β=-0.083, P=0.260）。對於後皮質分數，亦觀察到經由後側cBF之顯著間接路徑（間接β=-0.061，95% CI -0.127至-0.006，佔總關聯15%），同時仍存在顯著殘餘直接效應（β=-0.36, P<0.001）。

## PubMed Abstract

Cognitive impairment in Parkinson's disease is closely associated with degeneration of the cholinergic system, yet the precise structure-function relationships underlying this link remain incompletely characterized. Short-latency afferent inhibition (SAI) is a widely used neurophysiological marker of cholinergic circuit integrity, but its specific anatomical substrate and relationship to domain-specific cognitive decline are not fully elucidated. This study integrated neurophysiology, structural MRI and cognitive data to characterize the association between cholinergic basal forebrain (cBF) atrophy, SAI and cognitive performance in Parkinson's disease. In this cross-sectional study, we enrolled 119 patients with Parkinson's disease spanning the cognitive continuum from normal cognition to dementia (mean age 63.9 ± 9 years) and 32 healthy controls. All participants underwent assessment of SAI and multimodal MRI for automated volumetry of anterior and posterior basal forebrain subregions and substantia nigra free-water mapping. Cognitive status was classified using the Parkinson's Disease Cognitive Rating Scale (PD-CRS), yielding scores for global cognition and fronto-subcortical and posterior-cortical functions. Regression models and bootstrap mediation analysis were used to examine the SAI-cognition association and decompose it into components shared with and independent of the cBF volume. SAI was reduced in patients compared with controls. Within the PD group, after adjustment for clinically relevant covariates, SAI was significantly associated with PD-CRS total (β = -0.19, P = 0.013) and posterior-cortical scores (β = -0.44, P < 0.001), but not with the fronto-subcortical score (β = -0.13, P = 0.103). A mediation analysis identified a significant indirect path from SAI to global cognition through the posterior cBF volume [indirect β = -0.071, 95% confidence interval (CI) (-0.158, -0.013); 42% of the total association], with the residual direct path no longer statistically significant after inclusion of the mediator (β = -0.083, P = 0.260). A significant indirect path through the posterior cBF was additionally observed for the posterior-cortical score [indirect β = -0.061, 95% CI (-0.127, -0.006); 15% of the total association], alongside a substantial residual direct effect (β = -0.36, P < 0.001), consistent with SAI capturing aspects of the cholinergic system relevant to posterior-cortical performance beyond macroscopic cBF atrophy alone. These findings are consistent with SAI reflecting cholinergic circuit integrity linked to cBF structural status. The observed pattern of association, in which the posterior cBF volume captures a substantial proportion of the SAI-cognition covariance, positions SAI as a candidate functional marker for tracking cholinergic contributions to cognitive decline in Parkinson's disease, pending longitudinal validation.
