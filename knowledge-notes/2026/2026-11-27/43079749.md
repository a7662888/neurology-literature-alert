## PubMed Abstract

OBJECTIVES: Cellular metabolic dysfunction has emerged as a key contributor to neurodegeneration, but its role in Lewy body disease (LBD), particularly during prodromal stages, remains unclear. We investigated targeted blood-based proteomic profiles related to metabolic pathways across the REM sleep behavior disorder (RBD)-LBD continuum, and explored their potential as biomarkers of disease severity and phenoconversion.

METHODS: In this prospective cohort study, we enrolled 116 patients across the RBD-LBD continuum, including 47 with polysomnography-confirmed isolated RBD (iRBD), 45 with Parkinson's disease (PD) with RBD, and 24 with dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB) with RBD, along with 30 age-matched healthy controls. Plasma concentrations of 92 metabolism-related proteins were measured using a proximity extension assay. Motor function, neuropsychological assessments, and brain 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography imaging were obtained. Patients with iRBD were prospectively followed for up to 4 years to monitor phenoconversion.

RESULTS: Compared with controls, 16 differentially expressed metabolism-related proteins were identified across the RBD-LBD continuum, with predominant enrichment in neutrophil degranulation pathways. These protein alterations were mainly observed in patients with clinically manifest LBD, with DOPA decarboxylase demonstrating the most prominent differential expression and significant correlations with levodopa equivalent daily dose. In contrast, only N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) was significantly upregulated in patients with iRBD. Elevated NT-proBNP levels were associated with orthostatic intolerance, increased PD- and DLB-related metabolic brain pattern expressions, and earlier phenoconversion.

INTERPRETATION: Plasma metabolic proteins showed stage-specific alterations across the RBD-LBD continuum. NT-proBNP may have potential as a marker of short-term phenoconversion risk in iRBD. ANN NEUROL 2026.
