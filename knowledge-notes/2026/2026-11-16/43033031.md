## PubMed Abstract

BackgroundEarly identification of Alzheimer's disease (AD) remains challenging. Mild depressive symptoms (MDSs) during the prodromal stage accelerate cognitive decline, but the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Neuroinflammation and neurotrophic support are implicated in cognitive impairment, yet the interaction between triggering receptor expressed on myeloid cells 2 (TREM2)-mediated neuroimmune dysregulation and brain derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)/tropomyosin receptor kinase B (TrkB) deficits has not been established.ObjectiveThis study aimed to investigate whether alterations in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) TREM2/sTREM2 and BDNF/TrkB interact to affect cognition in patients with AD and MDSs and whether tau pathology mediates this relationship, using observational data from the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI).MethodsADNI data collected from 2005 to 2013 included 653 eligible participants. CSF TREM2, sTREM2, BDNF, TrkB, Aβ42, pTau181, and tTau levels, neuropsychiatric measures, and cognitive scores were analyzed using analysis of covariance, stratified analyses, mediation analyses, and multivariable linear regression adjusted for age, sex, education, and APOE ε4 status.ResultsCSF BDNF, TREM2, and sTREM2 levels varied significantly with age. sTREM2 appeared protective in early but not late disease stages. Reduced BDNF/TrkB signaling was associated with MDSs and poorer memory and executive function. Mediation analyses indicated that MDS-related cognitive impairment was partly mediated by TREM2/sTREM2-associated neuroinflammation and BDNF/TrkB dysfunction, particularly with elevated tau pathology.ConclusionsTREM2-mediated neuroimmune dysfunction and impaired BDNF/TrkB support may jointly exacerbate tau-related cognitive decline in preclinical AD with MDSs. This immune-neurotrophic interaction may promote dysfunctional microglial activation, excessive inflammation, and reduced clearance, offering a potential mechanism linking depressive symptoms to accelerated AD progression and informing early risk stratification and targeted intervention.
