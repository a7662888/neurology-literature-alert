## PubMed Abstract

Spinocerebellar ataxia type 2 (SCA2) is one of the most extensively investigated hereditary ataxias worldwide. Although the first clinical description of the disorder was reported in India in 1971 by Wadia and Swami, the province of Holguín, Cuba subsequently became recognized as a major geographical focus of SCA2, with a particularly high concentration of affected families and individuals, where it became internationally recognized as Cuban ataxia. Since the late 1970s, Cuban investigators have played a pivotal role in the clinical, epidemiological, neuropathological, and genetic characterization of the disorder. This article presents a historical review of the scientific development of Cuban ataxia based on a comprehensive bibliographic and documentary analysis, complemented by interviews with key neurologists involved in its early investigation. Particular emphasis is placed on the pioneering contributions of José Rafael Estrada González, Lionel Valles Calaña, Guillermo Orozco Díaz, and Luis Velázquez Pérez, whose work established the foundations for the current understanding of SCA2. Their investigations culminated in the identification of one of the world's largest SCA2 clusters and substantially advanced knowledge of the disease's clinical spectrum, epidemiology, and molecular genetics. The Cuban experience illustrates how sustained clinical observation, multidisciplinary collaboration, and international partnerships transformed a regional neurological disorder into one of the best-characterized hereditary ataxias worldwide. © 2026 International Parkinson and Movement Disorder Society.
