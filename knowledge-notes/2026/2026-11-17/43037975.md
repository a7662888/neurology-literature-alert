## PubMed Abstract

IMPORTANCE: The risk of Parkinson disease (PD) due to Agent Orange, the Vietnam War Era defoliant, is inferred from studies of agricultural pesticide exposure. Epidemiological work is currently lacking.

OBJECTIVE: To examine the association between Agent Orange exposure with incident cases of synucleinopathies including Parkinson disease, rapid eye movement (REM) sleep behavior disorder (RBD), and dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB) among US veterans.

DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS: This was an electronic health record-based cohort study inclusive of medical records from January 1, 1999, to December 30, 2022. Veterans were followed up for a mean (SD) of 12.5 (7.0) years from the start of records. Analyses were conducted from July 2024 to July 2026. The setting was the US Department of Veterans Affairs and included US veterans who served during the Vietnam War Era (August 1964 to May 1975) were screened. Those with incomplete demographic data or prevalent synucleinopathies before or within 6 months of follow-up were excluded.

EXPOSURE: Agent Orange defined by self-report. Direct or occupational exposure was defined by involvement in Operation Ranch Hand, Army Chemical Corps, and/or direct handling or spraying.

MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: Cumulative incidence of synucleinopathies was calculated adjusting for competing risk of death, age, traumatic brain injury, and frailty.

RESULTS: After screening of 4 917 272 US veterans, 4 468 509 veterans (mean [SD] age, 60.6 [8] years; 4 380 139 male [98%]) were included in analyses. A total of 1 035 210 veterans (23.2%) had Agent Orange exposure; 7600 (0.2%) of these had direct or occupational exposure. Birth year, age at time 0 (start of records), sex, race, ethnicity, and smoking history were balanced among the exposed and unexposed groups with further adjustment for traumatic brain injury and frailty. Agent Orange exposure was associated with higher risk of PD (hazard ratio [HR], 1.44; 95% CI, 1.37-1.52), RBD (HR, 1.78; 95% CI, 1.68-1.89), and DLB (HR, 1.66; 95% CI, 1.32-2.09). Associations strengthened when enriched for direct or occupational exposure, including PD (HR, 1.99; 95% CI, 1.71-2.32) and RBD (HR, 2.25; 95% CI, 1.87-2.70). Results were robust to additional adjustment for traumatic brain injury, frailty, health care utilization, and multiple imputation. Sensitivity analyses indicated that substantial unmeasured confounding would be required to explain the observed associations.

CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: Results of this cohort study suggest that Agent Orange may be an independent risk factor for later development of PD and related conditions. This finding has implications for global policy change. Further studies are needed to understand the mechanism underlying this association, gene-toxin interactions, and whether there is a unique clinical phenotype to those exposed to Agent Orange.
