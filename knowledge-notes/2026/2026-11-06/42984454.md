## 核心發現（Key Findings）

PD患者在邊緣系統與皮質下區域呈現較對照組更明顯的體積流失。PCA分析顯示一條後側皮質萎縮軸（PC3），此軸與區域粒線體密度呈正相關，且在LRRK2-PD患者中較偶發性PD患者表現更強。此萎縮軸與視覺空間功能障礙具有選擇性關聯。轉錄體驗證顯示萎縮程度較高的腦區傾向較高表現粒線體相關基因，其中粒線體核糖體組裝、粒線體DNA維持及輔酶Q生合成等路徑為最強的預測因子。

## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND: Mitochondrial dysfunction has been implicated in the pathobiology of Parkinson's disease (PD), yet the spatial relationship between regional energetic demand and patterns of neurodegeneration remains poorly understood, limiting mechanistic insight and development of disease-modifying therapies.

OBJECTIVES: This study tested whether regional mitochondrial density aligns with patterns of brain atrophy in PD, how this alignment differs across genetic groups, and whether the mitochondrial-atrophy axis associates with motor and cognitive features.

METHODS: Structural magnetic resonance imaging data were analyzed from 849 individuals (210 healthy controls, 639 patients with PD). Principal component analysis (PCA) identified dominant atrophy axes and tested their association with regional mitochondrial density and complex I activity. Transcriptomic validation used Allen Human Brain Atlas gene expression data restricted to 924 MitoCarta-annotated genes across 31 cortical regions.

RESULTS: Participants with PD exhibited higher volumetric loss in limbic and subcortical regions than controls. PCA showed a posterior cortical axis of atrophy (PC3) that correlated positively with regional mitochondrial density and was more strongly expressed in LRRK2-PD than in sporadic PD. This axis was selectively associated with visuospatial dysfunction. Transcriptomic validation confirmed that regions of greater atrophy preferentially expressed mitochondrial genes, with mitoribosome assembly, mitochondrial DNA maintenance, and coenzyme Q biosynthesis emerging as the strongest pathway-level predictors.

CONCLUSION: These findings link the brain's regional mitochondrial landscape to spatially patterned neurodegeneration in PD, with amplification in LRRK2 mutation carriers. This mitochondrial atrophy axis may provide a biologically informed marker aiding patient stratification and mitochondrial-targeted therapeutic development. © 2026 The Author(s).
