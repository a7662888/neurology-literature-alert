## 核心發現（Key Findings）

iRBD患者具有較低的健康型植物性飲食指數（調整後OR 0.48，95%CI 0.29-0.80，P=0.005），且較常攝取不健康植物性食物（精緻穀物、甜食與甜味劑）。飲食與腸道菌叢交互分析發現84.3%的iRBD相關腸道菌種與飲食因子有關，其中健康型PDI與Clostridium sp. AF20-17LB共現。縱向分析顯示4項飲食因子與5種iRBD相關菌種與神經退化結果有關；較高咖啡攝取與較低轉化風險相關（調整後HR 0.30，95%CI 0.11-0.78，P=0.01）。中介分析顯示iRBD富集菌種Ruthenibacterium lactatiformans可能中介不健康型PDI與以失智症為首發症狀之轉化間的關聯。

## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND: Accumulating evidence indicates that habitual diet, including plant-based dietary patterns, is associated with neurodegenerative α-synucleinopathy risk. However, the role of diet and its correlation with the gut microbiome in prodromal α-synucleinopathy, specifically among patients with isolated rapid eye movement (REM) sleep behavior disorder (iRBD), remains unclear.

OBJECTIVE: To evaluate dietary patterns, their interplay with gut microbial dysbiosis, and longitudinal effects on neurodegenerative progression in iRBD patients.

METHODS: We recruited 287 iRBD patients and 208 age- and sex-matched controls. Plant-based diet indices (PDIs) were derived from food frequency questionnaires. Shotgun metagenomic sequencing was performed in 456 subjects for diet-microbiome analyses. After a median follow-up of 4.2 years, 45 (15.7%) iRBD patients converted to α-synucleinopathies. Cox proportional hazards models assessed the associations between diet and neurodegenerative outcomes.

RESULTS: We found that iRBD patients exhibited lower healthful PDIs (adjusted odds ratio [aOR] 95% confidence interval [95% CI]: 0.48 [0.29-0.80], P = 0.005) but higher consumption of unhealthful plant-based foods (refined grains, sweets, and sweeteners). Diet-microbiome interaction analysis identified dietary associations for 84.3% of iRBD-related gut microbes, including co-occurrence between healthful PDI and Clostridium sp. AF20-17LB. Longitudinally, four dietary factors and five iRBD-related microbes were associated with neurodegenerative outcomes; specifically, higher coffee consumption was linked to a lower risk of progression to α-synucleinopathy (aHR [95% CI]: 0.30 [0.11-0.78], P = 0.01). Furthermore, mediation analyses indicated that the iRBD-enriched species Ruthenibacterium lactatiformans mediated the association between unhealthful PDI and dementia-first conversion.

CONCLUSIONS: Habitual diet in iRBD is associated with gut microbiome dysbiosis and predicts neurodegenerative progression. These findings highlight diet-microbiome interactions as modifiable targets for intervention in prodromal α-synucleinopathy. © 2026 The Author(s).
