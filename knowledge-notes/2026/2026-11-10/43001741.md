## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND: Whether intensive blood-pressure control reduces cardiovascular risk after cerebrovascular events remains uncertain.

METHODS: In this multicenter, open-label, randomized trial conducted at 30 sites in Brazil, we assigned participants with elevated blood pressure and previous ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack to receive intensive treatment targeting a systolic blood pressure of less than 120 mm Hg or to receive standard treatment targeting a systolic blood pressure of less than 140 mm Hg. The primary outcome was a composite of death from cardiovascular causes, nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal stroke, or hospitalization for unstable angina or heart failure.

RESULTS: A total of 4368 participants were enrolled, with 2172 assigned to intensive treatment and 2196 to standard treatment. The mean (±SD) age of the participants was 63.9±11.2 years, and 2093 (47.9%) were women. During the follow-up period, excluding the first 3 months, the mean systolic blood pressure was 122.0±11.5 mm Hg (median, 119.6 mm Hg) in the intensive-treatment group and 135.2±9.7 mm Hg (median, 135.0 mm Hg) in the standard-treatment group, with a between-group mean difference of 13.1 mm Hg (95% confidence interval [CI], 12.5 to 13.8). Over a median follow-up of 3.9 years, a primary-outcome event occurred in 236 participants (10.9%; 3.25 events per 100 person-years) with intensive treatment and in 297 (13.5%; 4.11 events per 100 person-years) with standard treatment (hazard ratio, 0.79; 95% CI, 0.67 to 0.94; P = 0.007). The composite renal outcome (sustained reduction of ≥50% in the estimated glomerular filtration rate or progression to end-stage renal disease) was more common with intensive treatment. The overall incidence of serious adverse events was similar in the two groups.

CONCLUSIONS: Among participants with previous ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack, targeting a systolic blood pressure of less than 120 mm Hg resulted in a lower incidence of major cardiovascular events than targeting a systolic blood pressure of less than 140 mm Hg. (Funded by Hospital Israelita Albert Einstein and the Brazilian Ministry of Health; OPTIMAL-STROKE ClinicalTrials.gov number, NCT04036409.).
