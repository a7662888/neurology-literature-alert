## 臨床意義（Clinical Relevance）

本統合分析基於隨機對照試驗資料，顯示降血壓治療與重大心血管事件風險降低有關，此關聯在涵蓋低風險（預測5年風險低於4%）至高風險族群中均觀察到。然而高風險族群的絕對風險降幅較大、NNT較低，顯示治療效益之臨床意義在不同風險族群間仍有差異；同時降血壓治療伴隨心血管/血流動力學、腎臟及神經前庭/跌倒相關不良事件發生率增加，臨床應用需權衡個別病患風險與治療副作用。

## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND: Treatment for lowering blood pressure reduces the risk of cardiovascular events among persons at high risk. Whether this benefit extends to those at lower risk remains unclear.

METHODS: We performed a meta-analysis of individual participant data from randomized trials comparing antihypertensive treatment (intervention) with placebo, an active comparator, or usual care (comparator). The primary outcome was the first occurrence of a major cardiovascular event (fatal or nonfatal stroke, fatal or nonfatal ischemic heart disease, or heart failure resulting in death or hospitalization; composite outcome). Safety was assessed in trials that compared antihypertensive treatment with placebo or usual care, according to three prespecified composite outcomes: cardiovascular or hemodynamic events (palpitations, bradycardia, or hypotension), adverse renal events, and neurovestibular or fall-associated events. Participants were grouped into deciles according to predicted 5-year risk of a major cardiovascular event (median, 12.3%; lowest decile, <4%; highest decile, >27%).

RESULTS: We included data from 51 trials and 357,440 participants - 169,042 in the intervention group and 188,398 in the comparator group. Over a median follow-up of 4.2 years, a major cardiovascular event occurred in 19,202 participants (11.4%) in the intervention group and in 24,398 (13.0%) in the comparator group. The overall estimated hazard ratio for a major cardiovascular event per 5-mm Hg reduction in systolic blood pressure was 0.90 (95% confidence interval, 0.88 to 0.92), with no evidence of variation across deciles. The absolute reduction in the 5-year cumulative risk of a major cardiovascular event was greater in the highest decile than in the lowest decile (2.6 vs. 0.4 percentage points), and the number needed to treat to prevent one major cardiovascular event over 5 years was lower in the highest decile than in the lowest decile (38 vs. 255). The incidence of a cardiovascular or hemodynamic event was 12.0 per 1000 person-years in the intervention group and 6.5 per 1000 person-years in the comparator group; the incidence of an adverse renal event was 3.3 and 2.2 per 1000 person-years, respectively; and the incidence of a neurovestibular or fall-associated event was 19.1 and 16.6 per 1000 person-years. The relative increase in the risk of each safety-outcome event with antihypertensive treatment as compared with placebo or usual care did not appear to vary across deciles.

CONCLUSIONS: Blood-pressure lowering was associated with a decreased risk of major cardiovascular events across the risk spectrum, including a predicted 5-year risk below 4%. (Funded by the British Heart Foundation; PROSPERO registry number, CRD42018099283.).
