## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND: Spinocerebellar ataxia type 3 (SCA3) exhibits marked clinical heterogeneity unexplained by expanded ATXN3 CAG repeat length alone. Polymorphic CAG repeats in several polyglutamine disease genes are established SCA3 modifiers.

OBJECTIVE: To determine whether THAP11 CAG repeat length modifies SCA3 clinical heterogeneity, including age at onset (AO) and disease progression.

METHODS: We analyzed data from 1265 genetically confirmed SCA3 mutation carriers (1038 affected, 227 presymptomatic). Linear regression, Cox models, and spline analyses evaluated associations between THAP11 CAG repeat length and AO. Robust regression and sensitivity analyses assessed THAP11 effects on baseline ataxia severity and disease progression. Complementary cellular assays assessed how THAP11 variants with distinct polyglutamine tract lengths affect ataxin-3 homeostasis.

RESULTS: THAP11 CAG repeat length was not associated with AO. However, THAP11 CAG status significantly interacted with expanded ATXN3 CAG repeat length to modify both baseline ataxia severity (β = 0.087, P = 0.011) and disease progression (β = 0.118, P = 0.026), with THAP11 > 29 repeats associated with lower predicted severity and slower progression at lower expanded ATXN3 CAG repeat lengths. Mechanistically, THAP11-38Q preferentially reduced ataxin-3 with shorter polyglutamine tracts in association with increased eIF2α phosphorylation. Integrated stress response inhibitor (ISRIB) abolished this effect, which was attenuated with longer polyglutamine tracts.

CONCLUSIONS: THAP11 CAG repeat length acts as a context-dependent genetic modifier of SCA3 through gene-gene interactions rather than exerting an independent effect. The selective responsiveness of ataxin-3 with shorter polyglutamine tracts to THAP11-associated reduction provides a potential biological explanation for the clinical interaction pattern. © 2026 International Parkinson and Movement Disorder Society.
