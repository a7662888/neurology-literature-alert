## PubMed Abstract

Hemichorea is a rare movement disorder that can arise from a diversity of causes. In this case, a 7-year-old boy presented with subacute onset of involuntary movements involving the left upper extremity. This case highlights the importance of careful characterization of abnormal movements in children, as distinguishing chorea from myoclonus, tics, or epileptic automatisms directs subsequent diagnostic testing. It also underscores the broad differential diagnosis of hemichorea in pediatric patients and the need to consider systemic and vascular processes when imaging reveals focal findings. Through stepwise clinical reasoning, this case illustrates how integrating movement phenomenology with neuroimaging and systemic evaluation can uncover an underlying unifying diagnosis.
