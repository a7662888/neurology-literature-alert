## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND: Tau aggregation is a defining feature of the pathophysiology of Alzheimer's disease and an appealing therapeutic target, yet tau-directed antibodies tested to date-primarily targeting N-terminal epitopes-have failed to slow tau progression or cognitive decline. Bepranemab targets the tau mid-region, predicted in cell-based assays to more effectively block neuron-to-neuron tau spread than anti-tau antibodies targeting the N-terminus. We aimed to investigate the safety and efficacy of bepranemab in slowing progression of tau pathology and clinical decline in people with prodromal-to-mild Alzheimer's disease.

METHODS: This double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 2 trial recruited participants across 101 outpatient sites in Europe (eight countries), Canada, and the USA. Participants (aged 50-80 years) with prodromal-to-mild Alzheimer's disease were randomly assigned 1:1:1 (using an interactive response system) to intravenous bepranemab 45 mg/kg, bepranemab 90 mg/kg, or placebo (every 4 weeks for 76 weeks), stratified by diagnosis, apolipoprotein E ɛ4 carrier status, and symptomatic treatment use. The primary endpoint was change from baseline to week 80 in Clinical Dementia Rating Scale-Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB) total score. Efficacy was assessed in the full analysis set (ie, all randomly assigned participants who received at least a partial dose of trial medication and had at least one valid post-baseline clinic visit and efficacy assessment). Safety was assessed in all randomly assigned participants who received at least a partial dose of the trial medication. No imputation was done for missing data. The trial is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04867616) and is complete.

FINDINGS: Between Aug 24, 2021, and Oct 25, 2022, 466 participants were randomly assigned to bepranemab 45 mg/kg (n=154), bepranemab 90 mg/kg (n=155), or placebo (n=157) at 85 sites. Median age was 72·0 years (IQR 66·0-76·0), 259 (56%) were female, and 207 (44%) were male. In total, 460 participants were included in the full analysis set: bepranemab 45 mg/kg (n=152), bepranemab 90 mg/kg (n=152), and placebo (n=156). Least-squares mean CDR-SB total score change from baseline to week 80 was 2·15 in the bepranemab 90 mg/kg group and 1·85 in the bepranemab 45 mg/kg group versus 1·81 in the placebo group, with differences versus placebo of 0·34 (95% CI -0·09 to 0·78, p=0·12; 80% CI 0·06 to 0·63) for the bepranemab 90 mg/kg group and 0·04 (-0·40 to 0·47, p=0·87; -0·25 to 0·32) for the bepranemab 45 mg/kg group. Frequency of treatment-emergent adverse events with bepranemab was similar to the placebo group (139 [91%] with bepranemab 45 mg/kg, 140 [91%] with bepranemab 90 mg/kg, and 142 [90%] with placebo); no treatment-related neuroinflammatory safety signals were identified clinically or on brain MRI scans. There were adverse events leading to death in four (1%) participants in the combined bepranemab groups and six (4%) in the placebo group; none were considered related to the trial treatment.

INTERPRETATION: Treatment with bepranemab was associated with an acceptable safety profile. However, the primary endpoint was not met. Hypothesis-generating differences favouring bepranemab in tau PET and 14-item Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale-Cognitive Subscale should be tested prospectively in trials powered for tau PET outcomes and stratified by baseline tau burden and apolipoprotein E ɛ4 status.
