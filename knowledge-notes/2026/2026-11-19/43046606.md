## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND: This study aimed to evaluate the effects of multidomain interventions on dementia risk scores and cognitive composite scores and to explore the influence of study, participant, and intervention characteristics on effect estimates.

METHODS: Five electronic databases (PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, CINAHL, and PsycINFO) were systematically searched from inception to March 2026. Randomized controlled trials that assessed multidomain interventions on the outcomes of dementia risk scores and cognitive composite scores were included. The standardized mean difference (SMD) was calculated using random-effects models.

RESULTS: Twenty-seven randomized controlled trials comprising 20,751 participants were included. Multidomain interventions had small but significant effects on both dementia risk scores (SMD = -0.11; p < 0.001) and cognitive composite scores (SMD = 0.12; p < 0.001). Interventions combining physical activity, nutrition, cognitive training, and social engagement were associated with greater reductions in dementia risk scores (subgroup difference p = 0.018).

DISCUSSION: Multidomain interventions have a small but significant effect, reducing dementia risk scores and improving composite cognitive scores.
