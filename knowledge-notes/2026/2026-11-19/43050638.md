## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Migraine and smoking are established stroke risk factors. We examined the individual and combined effects of migraine and smoking on risk of premature stroke (age 60 years or younger) and all-cause mortality in women and evaluated additive interaction.

METHODS: We conducted a population-based cohort study using nationwide Danish health registries (1996-2022). Women aged 15-60 years giving birth during 1996-2022 were identified through the Danish Medical Birth Registry and categorized as having migraine, smoking, both exposures, or neither (reference). Participants were followed until stroke, death, emigration, 61st birthday, or December 31, 2022. Outcomes were ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, and all-cause mortality. Absolute risks (ARs), adjusted hazard ratios (HRs), and relative excess risk due to interaction (RERI) were estimated.

RESULTS: Among 1,677,707 women (median age 29-32 years across exposure groups), the 20-year AR of premature ischemic stroke was 0.8% in the reference cohort (n = 1,389,361), 1.3% with migraine (n = 65,422), 1.5% with smoking (n = 214,277), and 2.8% with both exposures (n = 8,647). ARs of premature hemorrhagic stroke were 0.3%, 0.5%, 0.6%, and 0.9%. All-cause mortality ARs were 1.0%, 1.2%, 2.3%, and 3.4%. Compared with the reference cohort, adjusted HRs for premature ischemic stroke were 1.85 (95% CI 1.66-2.05) for migraine, 2.10 (95% CI 1.99-2.20) for smoking, and 3.61 (95% CI 3.03-4.31) for both exposures. HRs for premature hemorrhagic stroke were 1.46 (95% CI 1.23-1.74), 2.02 (95% CI 1.87-2.18), and 2.60 (95% CI 1.91-3.54). For all-cause mortality, HRs were 1.08 (95% CI 0.96-1.22), 2.44 (95% CI 2.34-2.54), and 3.30 (95% CI 2.80-3.88). The 20-year RERI was 0.67 (95% CI 0.22-1.13) for ischemic stroke, 0.11 (95% CI -0.59-0.82) for hemorrhagic stroke, and 0.78 (95% CI 0.34-1.22) for mortality.

DISCUSSION: Migraine and smoking were associated with elevated risk of premature stroke, and mortality risk was increased among women who smoked regardless of migraine status. Additive interaction indicated excess risk of ischemic stroke and all-cause mortality among women with migraine who smoked. These findings support targeted cessation strategies to reduce premature vascular events and mortality.
