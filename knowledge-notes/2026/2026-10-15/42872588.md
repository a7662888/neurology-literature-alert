## 核心發現（Key Findings）

1) 自人腦分離之EV經蛋白質體學與免疫墨點法證實純度高，來源包含神經元與膠質細胞亞型；2) PD與DLB中，經CD81校正之總αSyn量，以及以聚集偏好性抗體檢測到之神經元（L1CAM+）EV中聚集型αSyn百分比均增加；3) MSA中，聚集型αSyn主要分布於神經元EV，少部分見於膠質細胞EV，但總EV相關αSyn量並未增加；4) dSTORM抗原決定位定位分析發現，針對αSyn第34-45號殘基表位之抗體所偵測到的特定構形，在MSA之L1CAM+ EV表面富集程度高於PD；5) 此N端免疫反應性構形亦可在血清中偵測到，且在MSA患者之L1CAM+ EV表面的富集程度高於PD患者或對照組。

## PubMed Abstract

Parkinson's disease (PD), dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB), and multiple system atrophy (MSA) are unified by pathological α-synuclein (αSyn) aggregation, yet diverge in terms of the cellular distribution of pathology and clinical phenotype. Extracellular vesicles (EVs) have emerged as potential mediators or biomarkers of αSyn pathology, but whether their molecular constituents in the brain reflect disease-specific processes or conformations remains unknown. Here, we combined EV isolation, electrochemiluminescence, quantitative mass spectrometry and nanoscale single-vesicle imaging by direct stochastic optical reconstruction microscopy (dSTORM) to map the topology, epitope exposure and cellular origin of αSyn in EVs isolated from post-mortem PD, DLB, MSA, and healthy control (HC) brains and assessed the tractability of these molecular signatures in blood. Proteomics and immunoblotting of EV lysates isolated from human brains confirmed that they were highly purified and derived from neurons and glial cell subtypes. Across diseases, αSyn association with EVs exhibited distinct patterns: In PD and DLB, both total αSyn levels normalised to CD81 and the percentage of neuronal (L1CAM+) EVs with aggregated αSyn as detected by aggregate-preferring antibodies, were increased, whereas in MSA, aggregated αSyn was detected primarily in neuronal and to a lesser extent glial EV subpopulations without an increase in total EV-associated αSyn. Further epitope mapping by dSTORM identified a distinct αSyn conformational arrangement, as detected by antibodies against an epitope spanning αSyn residues 34-45, that was enriched on the surface of L1CAM+ EVs in MSA compared to PD. This N-terminal immunoreactive conformer was tractable in serum and enriched on the surface of L1CAM+ EVs from MSA compared to PD patients or controls. Collectively, our findings suggest that the epitope accessibility and topology of EV-associated αSyn proteoforms, rather than their abundance alone, discriminate synucleinopathy subtypes, offering a framework for a blood-based conformational stratification of these disorders.
