## PubMed Abstract

Brain myeloid cells, including infiltrating monocyte-derived macrophages (MDMs) and microglia, play critical roles in ischemic brain injury, clearing debris and shaping neuroinflammation and neurological outcomes. Yet the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Here, using single-cell RNA sequencing and fate mapping with an MDM-specific Ms4a3creERT2-Ai14 mouse line, we identified serotonin receptor Htr2b+ MDMs as a crucial cell population involved in resolving neuroinflammation within the ischemic penumbra. Htr2b+ MDMs significantly accumulate in the peri-infarct region and exhibit enhanced phagocytosis and inflammation-resolving gene signatures. Mechanistically, Htr2b activation promotes nuclear translocation of Tfe3, a key lysosomal regulator, enhancing lysosomal biogenesis and supporting phagocytic activity. MDM-specific Htr2b ablation impairs debris clearance, exacerbates neuroinflammation and ischemic injury, whereas pharmacological agonism or Tfe3 activation restores lysosomal biogenesis in brain MDMs and improves post-stroke outcomes. Thus we demonstrate a previously unrecognized Htr2b+ MDM subset that integrates serotonin signaling to license intrinsic phagocytic function, alleviating ischemic brain injury.
