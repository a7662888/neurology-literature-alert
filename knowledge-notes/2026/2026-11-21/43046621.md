## PubMed Abstract

INTRODUCTION: Commonly used brain health assessments rely on elevated levels of skill and address only one brain health domain.

METHODS: A modified e-Delphi of 17 international experts considered the concept, content, and face validity and dimensionality of Hachinski's proposal to combine self-assessed cognitive, psychological, and social domains of brain health. A moderator shared anonymized results with panelists, informing input on each round. A generative and three additional rounds reached consensus.

RESULTS: Three questions included cognitive (how is your mind working), psychological (how is your mood), and social health (how are your social relationships). Each item has a five-point descriptor (much worse, a bit worse, the same, a bit better, much better) for time set by clinical or study relevance and repeated at follow-up.

DISCUSSION: This study establishes the concept, content, and face validity of a self-assessed Integral Brain Health Index, offering a framework for construct validity, reliability, predictive validity, and clinical usefulness.
