## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Essential tremor (ET) is characterized not only by motor disturbances but also, in many cases, by a wide array of cognitive symptoms. Accumulating evidence implicates pathways beyond the cerebellum by which cognitive changes emerge. However, the extracerebellar neuropathologic correlates of specific cognitive symptoms have not been examined. This study evaluated these potential clinicopathologic associations in older adults with ET.

METHODS: Participants were adults ≥55 years with ET who were followed prospectively and longitudinally and donated their brains for neuropathologic evaluation as part of the Clinical-Pathological Study of Cognitive Impairment in Essential Tremor study. Cognitive outcomes included attention, executive function, language, memory, and visuospatial function. Postmortem neuropathologic assessment included Consortium to Establish a Registry for Alzheimer's Disease (CERAD) neuritic plaque scores, Thal β-amyloid A score, Braak and Braak neurofibrillary tangle (NFT) stage, and Braak Lewy pathology stage. Regional cerebral cortical atrophy ratings (neocortical and hippocampal), cerebral amyloid angiopathy, and markers of cerebrovascular health were also rated. Regression models examined neuropathologic correlates of cognitive function, adjusting for numerous confounders. Sensitivity analyses examined robustness of findings.

RESULTS: The sample consisted of 57 individuals with ET and without dementia (mean age = 88.6 ± 5.4 years; 54.4% female, 71.9% cognitively normal, 28.1% with mild cognitive impairment). Higher amyloid burden was associated with lower attention (CERAD β = -0.48, p < 0.001; Thal β = -0.37, p < 0.01) and lower executive function (CERAD β = -0.40, p 0.01; Thal β = -0.33, p = 0.01). CERAD score was associated with lower language (β = -0.31, p = 0.04). Braak NFT stage was associated with lower attention (β = -0.26, p = 0.04) and lower executive function (β = -0.28, p = 0.03). Hippocampal atrophy was associated with lower memory performance (β = -0.29, p = 0.04).

DISCUSSION: Core pathologies of AD, amyloid and tau, are associated with decrements in attention and executive function in older adults with ET and without dementia, while hippocampal atrophy is associated with lower memory performance. These results, consistent with a growing literature on extracerebellar pathologies in ET, suggest that multidomain cognitive symptoms among older adults with ET and without dementia may be driven in large part by AD-related cortical degenerative processes. Ongoing work is examining the molecular basis of these processes. Limitations include a highly educated, demographically homogeneous, and relatively small sample, as well as a lack of a control group.
