## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND/OBJECTIVES: The goal was to identify drivers of visual disability in Parkinson's disease (PD).

METHODS: Participants with PD (n = 25) and controls (n = 11) completed the National Eye Institute Visual Function Questionnaire (VFQ-25) and the Convergence Insufficiency Symptom Survey (CISS), underwent ophthalmologic examination, and high-resolution binocular eye tracking of vergence and gaze-holding. Predictive models were evaluated using clinic-only and combined clinic + eye-tracking approaches.

RESULTS: PD participants had lower VFQ-25, higher CISS scores, and the deficits centered on near activities and mental health. Eye tracking showed delayed vergence initiation, reduced gain, and greater gaze instability. Near-point convergence (NPC) and eye-tracking metrics correlated with VFQ-25 and CISS scores. Predictive performance was evident with clinical measures alone and improved with added eye-tracking variables.

CONCLUSIONS: Visual disability in PD is strongly linked to convergence insufficiency and gaze instability. NPC, particularly when paired with eye tracking, may be a practical marker of patient-reported visual outcomes. © 2026 International Parkinson and Movement Disorder Society.
