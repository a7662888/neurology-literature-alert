## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: APOE genotype is the strongest genetic risk factor for sporadic Alzheimer disease, but evidence on its utility in clinical practice is limited. We evaluated the predictive value of APOE genotype for PET-measured amyloid positivity and global amyloid burden in older adults with cognitive impairment evaluated in memory clinics as part of their clinical care. We also examined associations between APOE genotype and clinical presentation, including impairment level (mild cognitive impairment [MCI] or dementia) and MCI type (amnestic or nonamnestic).

METHODS: This observational multicenter study included Medicare beneficiaries with MCI or dementia from the Imaging Dementia-Evidence for Amyloid Scanning (IDEAS) study, who were coenrolled in the Amyloid Neuroimaging and Genetics Initiative (ANGI). Participants underwent amyloid PET and APOE genotyping. Amyloid status was determined using local visual reads and a Centiloid threshold (24.4). Logistic regression assessed associations between APOE genotype and amyloid positivity. Analysis of covariance models assessed differences in global amyloid burden (Centiloids) across genotypes. Voxel-wise analyses examined regional tracer uptake.

RESULTS: The study included 1,641 participants (mean [SD] age, 74.3 [5.6] years; 43.6% female), of whom 1,219 had MCI and 422 had dementia. APOE2/4, APOE3/4, and APOE4/4 genotypes had progressively higher odds of visual reads-based amyloid positivity compared with APOE3/3 (APOE2/4, OR = 3.10, 95% CI [1.58-6.47], p = 0.002; APOE3/4, OR = 4.52, 95% CI [3.50-5.86], p < 0.001; APOE4/4, OR = 26.34, 95% CI [12.93-63.39], p < 0.001). The results were similar when scan positivity was defined by a Centiloid threshold. Amyloid burden increased progressively across APOE genotypes, with higher-risk genotypes (APOE3/4 and APOE4/4) showing the highest Centiloids (p < 0.001, partial η2 = 0.20). Among amyloid-positive participants, APOE4 genotype was associated with increased tracer uptake across posterior cingulate, precuneus, medial frontal, temporoparietal, and lateral frontal cortices in a dose-dependent manner, whereas APOE2 genotype showed a more focal distribution. A significant APOE3/4-by-sex interaction indicated higher odds of visual reads-based amyloid positivity in APOE3/4 women. Clinically, APOE4/4 was associated with dementia and amnestic MCI.

DISCUSSION: APOE4 genotype was associated with both amyloid PET positivity and quantitative amyloid burden, following a dose-dependent gradient. These findings extend evidence linking APOE genotype to amyloid pathology in clinical settings. Limitations include restriction to cognitively impaired individuals and limited racial and ethnic diversity.
