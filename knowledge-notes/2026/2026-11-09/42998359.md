## PubMed Abstract

INTRODUCTION: Parkinson's disease (PD) is characterized by clinical heterogeneity and substantial variability in disease course. Insulin resistance (IR) has been proposed as a contributor to PD progression, although its prognostic role remains unclear. We investigated whether blood surrogate markers of IR, the triglyceride/high-density lipoprotein cholesterol ratio (TG/HDL-C) and triglyceride-glucose index (TyG), could predict the development of clinical milestones (CM) in early-stage PD patients (ePD).

MATERIALS AND METHODS: this longitudinal study included 98 ePD patients and 81 matched controls. At baseline, participants underwent clinical examination and blood sampling, allowing calculation of TG/HDL-C and TyG. Patients were followed for at least one year and stratified according to CM occurrence, defined as the first visit at which cognitive impairment, recurrent falls or visual hallucinations were documented. An Elastic Net-penalized Cox model with bootstrap resampling was used to identify robust prognostic variables while minimizing overfitting. Selected variables were then entered into a multivariable Cox proportional hazards model. Time-dependent ROC analysis assessed the prognostic performance of TG/HDL-C.

RESULTS: baseline TG/HDL-C levels were significantly higher among patients experiencing CM. Notably, this marker emerged as the only independent predictor of CM development in the multivariable analysis (HR = 2.03, p < 0.001). Higher values were also associated with reduced CM-free survival and showed moderate prognostic discrimination at 60 months (AUC = 0.737), with a cutoff of 1.63 identifying patients at increased risk of CM.

CONCLUSION: TG/HDL-C, a surrogate marker of IR, may provide prognostic information and help identify ePD patients at risk of worse disease progression.
