## 核心發現（Key Findings）

四種評分間一致性為中等程度（Spearman ρ = 0.39-0.60）；經二分類後各評分組間一致性為尚可至中等（Cohen's κ = 0.33-0.52）。保護性變項（教育年數≥10年占86%、社交／認知活動參與度達100%）出現天花板效應，使得心血管相關因子（BMI、高血壓、冠心病）成為解釋個體間變異的主要成分。儘管91%受試者BMI屬正常範圍（≤30 kg/m2），DEXA測得之體脂量仍於所有評分中呈現劑量反應梯度（CAIDE評分範圍4.36-6.26）；HbA1c亦顯示出自述糖尿病史所無法呈現之血糖梯度變化。

## 臨床意義（Clinical Relevance）

由於本研究缺乏實際認知結果資料，無法據此判斷這些風險評分在高社經族群中的預測效度或臨床應用價值；目前僅能說明評分間一致性為中等，且在此類健康篩選族群中保護性因子已趨飽和，評分變異主要來自心血管因子。此結果不可被解讀為評分工具在此族群中具有療效或預測效力之證明。

## 研究意義（Research Relevance）

研究結果顯示，在高社經、健康篩選族群中，現有認知衰退風險評分可能因保護性因子普遍偏高而降低其鑑別個體風險之能力，但摘要本身無法確定此現象是反映真實低風險，或是評分工具敏感度不足。輔助生物學測量（如體脂量、HbA1c）似乎能提供現有評分未能呈現之額外分層資訊，顯示未來或可發展結合生物標記之加強版風險評估工具，但仍需以實際認知結果驗證其效度。

## 批判（Strengths & Limitations）

本研究樣本為自我轉介、自費參與之高社經健康篩選族群，可能與一般族群特性不同，結果外推性有限。研究缺乏實際認知結果資料，因此無法評估評分之預測效度，亦無法判斷觀察到的保護因子天花板效應是反映真實低風險或評分工具侷限。摘要未提供長期追蹤或因果推論之設計，所有關聯僅能視為橫斷面觀察結果，不得解讀為因果或療效證據。

## PubMed Abstract

BackgroundValidated cognitive decline and Alzheimer's disease risk scores (CAIDE, ANU-ADRI, LIBRA, LIBRA2) were developed in general-population cohorts; how they behave in the high-socioeconomic, health-selected populations typical of preventive-medicine programs and over-represented in ageing cohorts is undescribed.ObjectiveLacking a cognitive outcome, we did not test predictive validity but described score distributions, inter-score concordance, the components driving variance, and complementary biological data.MethodsFour scores were computed for 895 participants aged ≥45 years in the Zoī cohort, a self-referred, self-funded preventive-medicine sample. Concordance was assessed by Spearman correlations and, after dichotomization, by pairwise agreement (percentage agreement, Cohen's κ). Principal component analysis identified components accounting for most between-person variance. Complementary data (DEXA body composition, HbA1c, erythrocyte omega-3) were cross-tabulated with score-derived risk categories.ResultsConcordance was moderate (ρ = 0.39-0.60), and dichotomized categories were fairly-to-moderately concordant across families (Cohen's κ 0.33-0.52). Protective variables showed ceiling effects (education ≥10 years, 86%; social/cognitive activity, 100%), leaving cardiovascular factors (BMI, hypertension, coronary heart disease) to account for most variance. Despite 91% having normal BMI (≤30 kg/m2), DEXA-measured fat mass showed a dose-response gradient across all scores (CAIDE 4.36-6.26); HbA1c revealed a glycemic gradient invisible to self-reported diabetes.ConclusionsIn this high-socioeconomic cohort, the scores were moderately concordant and varied almost solely by cardiovascular components, protective factors being saturated. Whether this reflects uniformly low risk or reduced ability to separate individuals cannot be determined without a cognitive outcome. Complementary biological measurements added stratification, supporting augmented risk-assessment tools to be validated against cognitive endpoints.
