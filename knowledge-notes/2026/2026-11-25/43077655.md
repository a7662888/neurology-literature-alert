## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Epidemiologic studies suggest altered cancer susceptibility in Parkinson disease (PD), but the magnitude and biological basis of these associations remain unclear. We aimed to quantify cancer risk after PD diagnosis, identify cancer-specific patterns, and evaluate contributions of smoking and shared genetic mechanisms.

METHODS: This retrospective study integrated 3 Finnish cohorts: biobank-based FinnGen, nationwide register-based FINPARK, and the clinically validated Turku University Hospital cohort. PD cases were matched to controls by age and sex, and analyses included cancers diagnosed after the index date. Fine-Gray competing-risk and Cox proportional hazards models estimated hazard ratios (HRs) of incident cancer. Genetic analyses included Mendelian randomization (MR), linkage disequilibrium score regression (LDSC), polygenic risk score (PGS), and genome-wide association studies (GWAS).

RESULTS: The FinnGen, FINPARK, and Turku cohorts included 4,277 individuals with PD (42,770 matched controls), 21,105 (63,285), and 1,103 (1,103), respectively. Across cohorts (mean age at PD diagnosis, 68.7-70.8 years; 40%-46% female), PD was associated with a 31%-41% lower overall cancer risk (Fine-Gray HR range 0.59 [95% CI 0.55-0.65] to 0.69 [0.66-0.72]). The strongest reductions were observed for respiratory (HR range 0.11-0.42) and gastrointestinal cancers (HR range 0.37-0.64). These associations persisted in Cox and smoking-adjusted analyses. Genetic analyses used FinnGen (n = 520,210) and previous European GWAS meta-analyses. MR estimates were consistent with inverse associations between PD genetic liability and respiratory (OR 0.943 [0.900-0.985]), gastrointestinal (0.966 [0.932-0.999]), and skin cancers (0.967 [0.943-0.991]). LDSC demonstrated positive genetic correlations between PD and melanoma (rg = 0.194, p = 0.016) and breast cancer (rg = 0.161, p = 0.006). PD PGS was associated with respiratory (OR 0.959 [0.940-0.979]), gastrointestinal (0.974 [0.960-0.989]), genitourinary (0.984 [0.970-0.998]), skin (0.985 [0.973-0.997]), and breast cancers (1.016 [1.001-1.030]). GWAS identified shared susceptibility loci between PD and several cancers.

DISCUSSION: PD was associated with a substantially reduced incidence of most cancers, particularly respiratory and gastrointestinal cancers. Combined epidemiologic and genetic findings suggest that these inverse associations are not explained by smoking alone but may partly reflect cancer-specific shared genetic susceptibility. Residual confounding and detection bias cannot be fully excluded, and generalizability beyond European populations is uncertain.
