## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Physiotherapy is a crucial element of care strategies for parkinsonian syndromes, especially in atypical parkinsonian disorders (APDs), where levodopa response is limited. Preliminary studies in APDs have reported improvements in walking and motor functions; however, small sample sizes represented a common limitation because of the rare nature of these diseases. The Mobility in Atypical Parkinsonism: a Trial of Physiotherapy (MobilityAPP) aims to investigate the superiority of gait-focused physiotherapy (GPT) in improving self-paced gait speed, compared with standard physiotherapy (SPT).

METHODS: This double-blind, randomized controlled trial was conducted in 5 centers across Europe. Patients with multiple system atrophy, progressive supranuclear palsy, and Parkinson disease (PD) were recruited through outpatient clinics or from specialized wards. Patients were included if they had a Hoehn and Yahr stage below 4 and excluded if their medication had been adapted recently or ambulation was influenced by other preconditions. After a 1-week home monitoring of physical activity, participants were randomized (1:1) to inpatient GPT or SPT for 2 weeks, followed by 5 weeks of home-based training. The primary outcome was the pre-post change in self-paced gait speed, measured with wearable sensors during a standardized 2 × 10-m walking test. Secondary outcomes included clinical scores, patient-reported outcomes (PROMs), and digital mobility measures from daily life. Repeated-measures analysis of variance was used to assess primary and secondary outcomes.

RESULTS: Of 167 patients screened, 160 were included (mean age 67.4, 51.2% female) and 151 completed the study per protocol (85 with APD, 66 with PD; 76 GPT, 75 SPT). GPT was comparable to SPT in improving self-paced gait speed (95% CI -0.04 to 0.04; p = 0.949). However, positive changes in daily-life mobility metrics were observed that favored the GPT intervention. Furthermore, encouraging effects were observed in both study arms regarding balance and both patient and clinical global impressions of change.

DISCUSSION: This randomized controlled trial provides evidence for the effectiveness of physiotherapy in APD. Physiotherapy led to promising results on daily-life mobility, clinical scales, and PROMs. Although GPT was not superior to SPT, the intervention was safe, well tolerated, and contributed to a perceived improvement in functional disability. A ceiling effect associated with the high self-paced speed of participants with PD has been observed as a potential limitation.

TRIAL REGISTRATION INFORMATION: NCT04608604 (clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04608604), registered on October 23, 2020.

CLASSIFICATION OF EVIDENCE: This study provides Class I evidence that, in patients with PD and atypical Parkinsonian syndromes, GPT and SPT resulted in similar self-paced gait speeds.
