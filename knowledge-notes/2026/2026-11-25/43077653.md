## 核心發現（Key Findings）

在認知正常族群中，兩世代皆觀察到類澱粉蛋白生物標記與白質高訊號之統計交互作用，其聯合關聯性超過兩者個別關聯性之總和：協調世代整體認知（β=-0.042，95% CI -0.068至-0.016，p=0.002）、記憶與執行功能皆有此現象；ADNI整體認知（β=-0.081，95% CI -0.136至-0.026，p=0.004）與記憶亦然。在輕度認知障礙族群中，未偵測到交互作用，兩標記在多個認知領域（協調世代之整體認知、記憶、執行功能；ADNI之記憶、語言）各自獨立與認知退化相關，其餘領域僅類澱粉蛋白生物標記達顯著關聯。在失智症族群中，協調世代觀察到類澱粉蛋白生物標記與退化相關，而白質高訊號則無；ADNI兩者皆未達顯著，作者推測可能與追蹤時間較短有關。

## 臨床意義（Clinical Relevance）

## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Alzheimer disease (AD) and cerebral small vessel disease frequently coexist, and their hallmarks, amyloid pathology and white matter hyperintensities (WMHs), are each associated with cognitive decline. Whether these associations are independent or interactive remains unclear. This study investigated their joint associations with cognitive decline across the clinical continuum.

METHODS: This longitudinal study included 2 observational cohorts: the harmonization cohort, which recruited participants from Singaporean community and memory clinics, and the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI), a multisite study in North America. Participants underwent MRI, amyloid biomarker assessments (plasma phosphorylated-tau 217 or amyloid PET), and neuropsychological assessments. Linear mixed-effects models evaluated independent and interactive associations of the amyloid biomarker and WMHs with cognitive decline, stratified by clinical stage.

RESULTS: The study included 448 participants from harmonization (mean age 72.45 ± 8.13 years; 56% female) and 1,245 from ADNI (72.56 ± 7.35; 49% female). Among cognitively normal (CN) individuals, statistical interactions between the amyloid biomarker and WMHs were detected in both cohorts, with the joint association exceeding the sum of their individual associations: in harmonization, for global cognition (amyloid biomarker × WMHs × time β = -0.042, 95% CI -0.068 to -0.016, p = 0.002), memory and executive function; in ADNI, for global cognition (β = -0.081, -0.136 to -0.026, p = 0.004) and memory. At mild cognitive impairment (MCI), no interaction was detected; both markers were independently associated with decline in several domains (global cognition, memory, and executive function in harmonization; memory and language in ADNI), whereas an association was detected for the amyloid biomarker only in the remaining domains. At dementia, the amyloid biomarker was associated with decline in harmonization, while WMHs were not; neither reached significance in ADNI, likely reflecting the shorter follow-up.

DISCUSSION: The joint association of amyloid pathology and WMHs with cognitive decline was stage dependent: at CN, it exceeded the sum of their individual associations; at MCI, both markers were independently associated with decline; and at dementia, an association was detected for the amyloid biomarker only. Clinical stage and WMH burden may therefore warrant consideration when interpreting disease-modifying therapy responses. However, the observational design limits causal inference, and the interaction reported is statistical rather than necessarily biological.
