## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Brain pathologies are common in the elderly; however, their contribution to Alzheimer dementia risk and cognitive decline in those who die as centenarians remain unclear. This study examines the frequency of Alzheimer disease neuropathologic changes (ADNC), limbic-predominant age-related TDP-43 encephalopathy neuropathologic change (LATE-NC), and other brain pathologies and their association with Alzheimer dementia risk and cognitive decline in centenarians.

METHODS: This clinicopathologic study included older participants from the Religious Orders Study and Rush Memory and Aging Project, 2 ongoing clinicopathologic cohort studies of aging and dementia, who underwent annual clinical evaluations and autopsy. Participants were evaluated annually for cognitive status and followed for a mean (SD) of 11.4 (5.3) years before death. Neuropathologic evaluation included ADNC (and separately β-amyloid load and tangle density), LATE-NC, presence or absence of neocortical Lewy bodies (LBs), hippocampal sclerosis of aging (HS-A), primary age-related tauopathy (PART), and 5 cerebrovascular disease (CVD) pathologies including arteriolosclerosis, atherosclerosis, cerebral amyloid angiopathy, macroinfarcts, and microinfarcts. Multivariable regression and linear mixed-effects models were applied.

RESULTS: A total of 104 centenarians were included in this study. The mean (SD) age at death was 102 (1.9) years, 84% were female, 58% had Alzheimer dementia, 21% had mild cognitive impairment (MCI), and 21% had no cognitive impairment (NCI). All 3 clinical diagnostic groups exhibited pathologies, including any type of CVD (94%), ADNC (72%; 73% of them were intermediate and 27% of them were high), LATE-NC (58%, 30% of them exhibiting HS-A), HS-A (18%, 95% had LATE-NC), PART (28%), and neocortical LBs (11%). Mixed pathologies, particularly ADNC + LATE-NC + CVD, were common in the Alzheimer dementia group (52%) compared with NCI (36%) and MCI (23%). After adjusted for age at death, sex, education, and other brain pathologies, only LATE-NC and tangles (specifically intermediate/high ADNC with Braak stage V) were associated with more rapid decline in global cognition and increased odds of Alzheimer dementia.

DISCUSSION: Brain pathology, but not dementia, is nearly inevitable in those who die as centenarians. LATE-NC and tangles are the key contributors to cognitive decline. Future study investigating resilience factors may inform preventive strategies.
