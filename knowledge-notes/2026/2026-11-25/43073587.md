## PubMed Abstract

Neurons and their synaptic connections harbor numerous and diverse activities through functional organization, thereby enabling nervous system complexity. Within densely packed presynaptic terminals, there exists a mix of molecules, organelles and synaptic vesicles arranged as a set of distinct domains, each with a specific composition that enables discrete functional programs. For example, a subset of synaptic vesicles is made available at the active zone for immediate use, while the vast majority of vesicles are located away from this region and reserved. Likewise, synaptic vesicle recycling and proteolytic processes take place in domains away from the synaptic vesicle cluster. An emerging body of work has established that these functional domains are maintained and regulated by phase separation of molecules, that is, the formation of non-membrane-bound macromolecular condensates. Thus, characterizing the phase separation of molecules and organelles within the presynapse and its role in facilitating robust functional output, diversity and coordination within the presynaptic terminal is crucial for elucidating the vesicle trafficking mechanisms that underlie synaptic transmission.
