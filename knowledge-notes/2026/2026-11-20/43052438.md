## PubMed Abstract

A major goal in neuroscience is to map the transformation of information as it flows through the brain. For example, neurons at many levels of the visual hierarchy are attuned to motion direction1-5. However, links between motion-selective computations and cell types at various stages of the visual hierarchy remain unidentified. A key first step in delineating these relationships is to map the wiring diagrams that connect different motion-selective cell types. Here we establish a systematic, cell-type-specific circuit-mapping method, combining an anterograde trans-neuronal fluorescent tracer (mWGA-mCherry)6 with spatial transcriptomics (multiplexed error-robust fluorescence in situ hybridization (MERFISH))7,8 to establish TransA-MERFISH. We used TransA-MERFISH to comprehensively map the synaptic target cell types of four retinal ganglion cell subclasses in superior colliculus (SC) at cell-type resolution. These experiments unexpectedly revealed that retinal on-off direction-selective ganglion cells (ooDSGCs) innervate several direction-selective SC cell types, but do not innervate a SC cell type that was previously reported to display strong direction-selective responses. We validated these results using orthogonal approaches and demonstrated the sensitivity and specificity of TransA-MERFISH. Functional studies of SC activity before and after ablation of retinal direction-selective circuits confirmed that these computationally distinct, ooDSGC-dependent and -independent direction-selective cell types are commingled in SC. Together, our study establishes TransA-MERFISH as a robust framework for mapping wiring diagrams at the cell-type level and uncovers divergent computational mechanisms of motion detection in early visual processing.
