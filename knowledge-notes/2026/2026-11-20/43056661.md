## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Migraine with aura is recognized as a risk factor for acute ischemic stroke (AIS). However, the short-term risk of stroke, particularly within 90 days of hospitalization for migraine, has not been thoroughly evaluated on a national scale. This study aims to address this gap by examining the association between migraine with aura during admission and 90-day AIS readmission.

METHODS: Using the Healthcare Cost and Utilization Project's Nationwide Readmissions Database between 2016 and 2019, we identified patients in the United States who received a diagnosis of migraine during admission, either principal or nonprincipal. The reference group for analysis contained all adult patients admitted during the study period without a principal or nonprincipal diagnosis of migraine. The primary outcome was subsequent admission for AIS within 90 days. Diagnoses were identified using standard and validated ICD-10-CM codes. Three patient groups were delineated: migraine with aura, migraine without aura, and unspecified migraine. Inverse probability of treatment weighting and multivariable Cox regression models were used to assess the risk of AIS across these 3 patient groups, adjusted for risk factors.

RESULTS: Among 64,808,935 patients (mean age 56.19 years, 60.0% female) admitted during the study period, 1,257,671 (1.94%) patients were admitted with any migraine diagnosis, of which 78,650 (6.25% of migraine cohort) were admitted with a principal diagnosis of migraine. In total, 75,460 (0.12%) were admitted with migraine with aura. Within 90 days, 272,812 (0.42%) patients were readmitted with AIS. After adjusting for cardiovascular risk factors, migraine with aura was associated with an elevated risk for 90-day AIS (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR] 1.98, 95% CI 1.68-2.35, p < 0.001). In the primary analysis, similar risk was not demonstrated in the overall migraine group (aHR 1.00, 95% CI 0.94-1.06, p = 0.960), migraine without aura group (aHR 0.86, 95% CI 0.62-1.21, p = 0.392), and unspecified migraine group (aHR 0.96, 95% CI 0.90-1.03, p = 0.257); however, modest associations emerged in sensitivity analyses.

DISCUSSION: Among hospitalized patients, migraine with aura was associated with an increased risk of AIS within 90 days. These findings underscore the importance of careful vascular risk assessment in this population. Future research should investigate the pathomechanisms underlying this association.
