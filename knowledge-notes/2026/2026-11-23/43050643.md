## PubMed Abstract

A 33-year-old woman with a medical history of polycystic ovary syndrome on combined oral contraceptive pills, insulin resistance, and bilateral silicone breast implants (SBIs) placed 10 years prior presented with a 2-month history of intermittent myoclonic jerks involving the head, trunk, and lower limbs; polyarthralgia; and cognitive and functional decline (compared with being previously independent in her activities of daily living). Family history was significant for epilepsy in her mother and 2 brothers. Neurologic examination revealed disorientation, significantly impaired short-term recall, visuospatial memory impairment, and a limited attention span. Her speech was characterized by frequent paraphasic errors and anomia; she also exhibited mild gait ataxia. Diagnostic studies ruled out common autoimmune diseases. Brain MRI and electroencephalography revealed no abnormalities, and CSF analysis was normal. Genetic testing for inherited prion disease and Huntington disease was negative, and serum and urinary copper levels were unremarkable. As part of a paraneoplastic workup, breast MRI revealed intracapsular rupture of both SBIs. The implants were surgically removed with complete capsulectomy. The patient's symptoms resolved completely by the seventh postoperative day. At the 6-month follow-up, she remained asymptomatic and had returned to her baseline functional status. Given the temporal association between implant rupture, explantation, and symptom resolution, the presentation was considered most consistent with a case of reversible rapidly progressive dementia secondary to the autoimmune/inflammatory syndrome induced by adjuvants.
