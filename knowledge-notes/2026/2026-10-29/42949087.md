## 核心發現（Key Findings）

統合分析顯示NM-MRI指標降低與下列臨床表現呈現相關：運動功能障礙加重（18篇研究，彙總r = -0.406）、疾病病程較長（14篇，r = -0.426）、Hoehn and Yahr分期較高（10篇，r = -0.336）、levodopa等效每日劑量較高（6篇，r = -0.337），各項關聯之P值均小於0.001。敘述性整合顯示SNpc與LC指標呈現不同關聯型態：SNpc指標主要與運動功能障礙及疾病分期相關，並呈現半球不對稱性、黑質內部地形分布差異及表現型特異性模式；LC指標則主要與認知、情緒、自律神經及睡眠相關症狀相關。四篇縱向研究（追蹤約1.5至3年）觀察到部分NM-MRI指標隨時間下降，年下降幅度約5%至19%。多模態研究提示NM-MRI可能捕捉到與多巴胺系統、鐵敏感影像及擴散影像指標互補之黑質紋狀體病理面向。

## PubMed Abstract

The neuropathology of Parkinson's disease (PD) is characterized in part by the degeneration of catecholaminergic neurons, particularly dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta (SNpc) and noradrenergic neurons in the locus coeruleus (LC), yet no validated in vivo biomarker quantifies this process. Neuromelanin-sensitive magnetic resonance imaging (NM-MRI) enables noninvasive assessment of these neuronal populations, although relationships with clinical severity, disease burden, and progression remain incompletely defined. We conducted the first Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA)-guided systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis examining associations between NM-MRI measures and clinical outcomes in PD, including subgroup, meta-regression, and sensitivity analyses. Reduced NM-MRI measures were associated with greater motor impairment (18 studies; pooled r = -0.406), longer disease duration (14; r = -0.426), higher Hoehn and Yahr stage (10; r = -0.336), and higher levodopa equivalent daily dose (6; r = -0.337) (all P < 0.001). Thirty-nine studies (1983 patients with PD) were included in the systematic review. Narrative synthesis demonstrated distinct SNpc/LC associations: SNpc measures were primarily associated with motor dysfunction and disease stage, displaying hemispheric asymmetry, intranigral topography, and phenotype-specific patterns, whereas LC measures were preferentially linked to cognitive, affective, autonomic, and sleep-related symptoms. Four longitudinal studies (~1.5-3 years) reported declines in selected NM-MRI measures (~5%-19% annually). Multimodal studies suggested NM-MRI captures a complementary dimension of nigrostriatal pathology alongside dopaminergic, iron-sensitive, and diffusion-based markers. NM-MRI provides a biologically interpretable in vivo measure of catecholaminergic neuron integrity and survival in PD, with consistent cross-sectional associations with clinical disease burden. Emerging longitudinal and multimodal evidence supports its potential as a progression-sensitive imaging biomarker for disease-modifying clinical trials. © 2026 The Author(s).
