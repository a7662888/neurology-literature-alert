## 核心發現（Key Findings）

研究推估至2050年，腦中風將影響約148百萬名男性中超過900萬人（6.1%）、約154百萬名女性中超過1040萬人（6.7%）。盛行率在黑人、美國原住民/阿拉斯加原住民及多重種族成人中最高，80歲以上族群可達25%至30%，相較之下同年齡白人成人為16%。西班牙裔與亞裔美國人的相對增幅最大，依年齡不同絕對病例數將增加2至6倍。低教育程度或低收入者及無健康保險者之盛行率仍將維持偏高；然而高中與大學畢業者及中高收入者之絕對病例數將約增加一倍，花費將增加5至8倍。經通膨調整後，45至64歲族群每年腦中風照護花費將增至五倍，達1420億美元。20至44歲年輕成人以及西班牙裔、美國原住民/阿拉斯加原住民與多重種族族群之花費增幅將超過1000%。

## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND: Given expected increases in stroke burden in the United States through 2050, forecasts of the burden by age, sex, race, ethnicity, education level, income level, and health insurance status are needed.

METHODS: We used National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (January 2015-March 2020) and Medical Expenditure Panel Survey (2015-2019) data to estimate recent trends in stroke prevalence and costs, then projected prevalence and costs through 2050 accounting for population sociodemographic changes. We also projected prevalence by age, sex, race, and ethnicity strata.

RESULTS: We estimate that stroke will affect >9.0 million of ≈148 million men (6.1%) and >10.4 million of ≈154 million women (6.7%) by 2050. Prevalence will be highest among Black, American Indian/Alaska Native, and multiracial adults, reaching 25% to 30% among those >80 years of age, compared with 16% among White adults of the same age. The largest relative increases will occur among Hispanic and Asian Americans, with a 2- to 6-fold increase in absolute case numbers, depending on age. Prevalence will remain high among people with lower levels of education or income and those without health insurance; however, absolute numbers will roughly double, and costs will increase 5- to 8-fold among high school and college graduates and middle- and high-income earners. Adjusted for inflation, annual stroke care costs will quintuple to $142 billion among people 45 to 64 years of age. Young adults 20 to 44 years of age and Hispanic, American Indian/Alaska Native, and multiracial populations will experience cost increases >1000%.

CONCLUSIONS: The alarming rise in stroke burden projected by 2050 is expected to affect sociodemographic groups unequally. Programs and policies to prevent and treat stroke across age groups, mitigate widening healthcare disparities, and reduce costs are urgently needed.
