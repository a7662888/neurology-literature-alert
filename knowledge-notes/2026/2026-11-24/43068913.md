## PubMed Abstract

Six years after the SARS-CoV-2 outbreak, we revisit the fear of a post-COVID parkinsonism wave, echoing encephalitis lethargica after the 1918 pandemic, through two questions. First, is the infection a risk factor for de novo Parkinson's disease (PD)? Epidemiology reveals no robust signal, yet pathological, imaging, and experimental evidence shows the virus targets the olfactory-brainstem axis, sustains neuroinflammation, promotes α-synuclein aggregation, and leaves a dopaminergic footprint. We hypothesize an inflammation-driven, rather than directly cytopathic, mechanism in vulnerable individuals, with persistent post-COVID hyposmia as a candidate risk marker. As the PD prodrome outlasts available follow-up, this question remains open. Second, does infection alter the trajectory of established PD? Motor and non-motor worsening during and shortly after COVID-19 is consistently demonstrated. However, accelerated neurodegeneration remains unproven. Neither alarm nor reassurance is warranted, but long-term, prospective, harmonized cohorts enriched for hyposmia and characterized with α-synuclein seed amplification assays, neurofilament light chain, and dopamine transporter imaging are needed. © 2026 International Parkinson and Movement Disorder Society.
