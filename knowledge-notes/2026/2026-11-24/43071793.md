## PubMed Abstract

Stroke remains the leading cause of long-term neurological disability worldwide, with limited treatment options. Increasing evidence shows that noncoding RNAs, including microRNAs, long noncoding RNAs, and circular RNAs, play crucial roles in shaping the poststroke pathophysiology. Beyond their individual effects, the crosstalk of noncoding RNAs regulates the poststroke pathophysiologic mechanisms. They form competing endogenous RNA networks enabled by direct base-pairing with other RNAs and scaffolding of RNAs with proteins. These interactions coordinate signals across molecular pathways, amplifying or buffering cellular responses to injury. Recent studies have shown that long noncoding RNAs and circular RNAs function as microRNA sponges and epigenetic scaffolds that regulate gene networks in a spatiotemporal manner. By evaluating structural features, mechanisms, and functional outcomes, this review highlights noncoding RNA crosstalk as a novel regulatory layer of poststroke brain damage. Targeting noncoding RNA crosstalk enables modulation of injury responses and provides insights for future RNA-based stroke therapies.
