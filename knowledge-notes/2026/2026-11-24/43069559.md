## PubMed Abstract

OBJECTIVE: Since the large language model (LLM) program Chat Generative Pre-trained Transformer (ChatGPT) was introduced in November 2022, the use of artificial intelligence (AI) expanded rapidly in published writing. This has been quantified in many fields, but not in medical journals that focus on neurology and neuroscience. We sought to characterize the use of LLM in articles submitted before and after ChatGPT became available to the general public, in a neurology journal, Annals of Neurology, that covers all neurology subspecialties.

METHODS: The study evaluated manuscripts submitted on movement disorders, dementia, and vascular neurology before and after ChatGPT became available. AI content was measured with LLM-detection platforms Pangram, Originality.ai and GPTZero. Substantial AI was defined as ≥20% of content, or >50% likelihood of AI use. Manuscript type, country of author, country, Human Development Index (HDI), AI disclosure, and publication decision were compared with AI detected.

RESULTS: Of 300 papers submitted after the introduction of ChatGPT, AI was detected by Pangram in 74 (25%) and this was substantial (≥20% of content) in 32 (11%). Substantial AI was more common in brief communications than full research articles (31% vs 10%, p = 0.006) and in vascular neurology articles (17%, vascular; 8%, dementia; and 5%, movement disorders; p = 0.012). Most manuscripts analyzed were submitted by authors from China (37%) and the United States (19%) and each had 11% substantial AI use. Authors from countries with lower levels of development were more likely to use AI (median HDI 0.797, AI ≥20% vs median HDI 0.925, AI <20%, p = 0.033). No AI was found in 60 papers submitted from July 3 to September 14, 2022 (pre-ChatGPT) by any of the AI-detection platforms. Of 300 post-ChatGPT manuscripts, 24 (8%) were accepted. None with substantial AI use were accepted (p = 0.090; odds ratio [OR] = 0.000, 95% confidence interval [CI] = 0.000-1.068). The authors of 5 (2%) of these papers disclosed AI use, including 1 (3%) of the papers with substantial AI detected.

INTERPRETATION: The study found AI was used in writing one quarter of papers submitted to Annals of Neurology after ChatGPT was introduced, with substantial AI content in 11%. No papers with AI ≥20% of content were accepted for publication. AI-generated text was more frequent in brief communications and vascular neurology articles. Authors in countries with lower levels of development were significantly more likely to use AI. The results suggest no advantage from AI in publication success, but point to a need for greater transparency by authors about AI use. ANN NEUROL 2026.
