## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND: Spinocerebellar ataxia type 8 (SCA8) is caused by a heterozygous (CTG·CAG)n repeat expansion in the two overlapping genes ATXN8OS/ATXN8 on chromosome 13q21, and its pathogenicity has been debated for more than 20 years.

METHODS: ExpansionHunter was used to screen ATXN8OS-oriented repeat expansions in short-read whole-genome sequencing data from 14,130 probands and 4307 healthy controls. Suspected expansions were validated by long-range polymerase chain reaction-based targeted high-fidelity (HiFi) sequencing, which also defined the normal allele range in 624 healthy individuals and characterized an independent replication cohort of 26 genetically confirmed SCA8 probands. Co-occurring pathogenic or likely pathogenic (P/LP) variants were screened in the SCA cohorts.

RESULTS: Targeted HiFi sequencing identified 207 samples with expanded repeats (range 71-1015) and 13 samples with intermediate repeats (range 59-70). No significant differences in carrier frequency or repeat size were observed among disease groups or between disease groups and controls, nor was a relationship observed between repeat size and age at onset. Sequence architecture analysis showed that 16.9% of expanded alleles were interrupted, with the four most common interruption types being CAG, CTT, CTC, and CCG, which was restricted to SCA patients and lay within the 100 to 300 repeat range. The majority of ATXN8OS expansion-positive SCA patients without CCG interruptions harbored co-occurring ataxia-related P/LP variants, whereas those with CCG interruptions carried no such variants.

CONCLUSIONS: Our findings demonstrate that CCG repeat interruptions, not repeat length, are the primary determinants of SCA8 pathogenicity, shedding new light on the long-standing debate over its clinical causality. © 2026 International Parkinson and Movement Disorder Society.
