# 急性失衡症候群與急性前庭症候群之臨床與病灶部位區別

## 核心發現（Key Findings）

75位中風相關急性眩暈/頭暈或失衡病人中，58.7%為AIS，38.7%為AVS，2.7%為中樞性姿勢性眩暈。AIS病灶核心位於小腦前葉與深部小腦核，經小腦上腳連接雙側橋腦中腦被蓋；AVS病灶位於小腦後葉/絨球小結葉，經小腦下腳連接同側前庭核及雙側內側縱束。SPN強度與小腦第VI-IX葉連接前庭核及內側縱束之體素相關；姿勢搖晃與小腦Crus I局部群聚相關。

## 機制（Mechanism）

摘要描述病灶症狀對應分析（lesion-symptom mapping）與結構斷連圖（disconnectome mapping）結果：AIS病灶主要位於小腦前葉及深部小腦核，透過小腦上腳與橋腦中腦被蓋相連，推測此網絡與姿勢及步態控制有關；AVS病灶主要位於小腦後葉/絨球小結葉，透過小腦下腳與前庭核及內側縱束相連，推測與前庭眼反射網絡有關。惟此為病灶部位與臨床表徵之關聯性分析，並非直接因果機制證實，摘要未提供更細緻之生理機轉說明。

## 臨床意義（Clinical Relevance）

摘要指出，目前常用於急性眩暈評估之臨床工具（如頭脈衝測試、凝視誘發眼震、偏斜檢查）主要針對有SPN之AVS病人設計，而本研究觀察到逾半數中風相關眩暈/頭暈或失衡病人屬於無SPN之AIS表現型。作者認為辨識AIS與AVS兩種臨床表現型，對於急診情境中辨識所有中風相關眩暈/頭暈病人具有重要性。此為觀察性研究結果，尚不能視為診斷流程改變之療效證明。

## 研究意義（Research Relevance）

本研究採用前瞻性急診世代設計，結合視訊眼震儀、姿勢儀及MRI病灶定位，並以支持向量回歸之病灶症狀對應分析與結構斷連圖方法，描繪AIS與AVS之病灶分佈差異，提供中風相關眩暈/頭暈表現型與病灶部位關聯之初步觀察證據，有助於未來研究進一步驗證不同表現型之病理解剖基礎。

## 教學應用（Teaching Application）

可用於教學說明中風相關急性眩暈/頭暈之兩種臨床表現型：AVS（伴SPN，病灶多在小腦後葉/絨球小結葉）與AIS（無SPN、以姿勢或步態失衡為主，病灶多在小腦前葉），並強調現行床邊前庭眼動檢查（HINTS）對AVS較敏感，但對AIS病人可能需輔以姿勢評估（如姿勢儀）以提高中風辨識率。

## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Stroke is a major differential diagnosis in patients with acute vertigo/dizziness or imbalance. Current diagnostic algorithms such as head impulse test, gaze-evoked nystagmus, and test of skew are optimized for presentations with spontaneous nystagmus (SPN), traditionally called acute vestibular syndrome (AVS). However, in recent studies, a relevant proportion of patients with stroke-related vertigo/dizziness had no SPN, but rather postural or gait imbalance, a phenotype lately termed acute imbalance syndrome (AIS). We aimed to outline the pathoanatomical differences of stroke-related AIS vs AVS and its clinical implications.

METHODS: Patients with MRI-proven stroke-related acute vertigo/dizziness or imbalance were included in this emergency department (ED) cohort study at LMU Hospital prospectively. Vestibular, ocular motor, and postural signs were documented (including SPN presence/intensity by video oculography, postural imbalance by posturography). Patients were classified as suffering from AIS or AVS based on the presence of SPN. Stroke lesion distribution was depicted in Montreal Neurological Institute space and supplemented by structural disconnectome mapping from the lesion cores. Multivariate lesion symptom-mapping based on support-vector regression and disconnectome mapping was applied for SPN intensity and sway path as surrogate markers for AVS and AIS.

RESULTS: Of 75 patients (age: 65.7 ± 13.3 years, 40% female), 58.7% were diagnosed with AIS, 38.7% with AVS, and 2.7% with central positional vertigo. In AIS, the lesion core was located in the anterior cerebellar lobe and deep cerebellar nuclei (superior cerebellar artery/posterior inferior cerebellar artery territory) connecting to the bilateral pontomesencephalic tegmentum through the superior cerebellar peduncle. AVS stroke lesions were mostly located in the posterior cerebellar/flocculonodular lobe (posterior inferior cerebellar artery/anterior inferior cerebellar artery territory) and connected to the ipsilesional vestibular nucleus (VN) and bilateral medial longitudinal fascicle (MLF) through the inferior cerebellar peduncle. SPN intensity correlated with voxels in the cerebellar lobules VI-IX connecting to VN and MLF. Postural sway was associated with a small local cluster in the cerebellar Crus I.

DISCUSSION: AIS accounts for more than half of patients with stroke-related vertigo/dizziness or imbalance. AIS lesions were located in the anterior cerebellar lobe, disturbing cerebellar-pontomesencephalic networks for posture and gait control. AVS lesions affected the posterior cerebellar/flocculonodular lobe and associated vestibulo-ocular reflex networks. Recognition of both clinical phenotypes is essential to identify all strokes among patients with vertigo/dizziness in the emergency setting.
