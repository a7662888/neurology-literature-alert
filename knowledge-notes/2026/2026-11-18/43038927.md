## PubMed Abstract

INTRODUCTION: We examined whether safinamide treatment persistence differed according to timing of initiation after motor fluctuation onset in Parkinson's disease (PD).

METHODS: We retrospectively evaluated 200 patients with PD treated with safinamide at two Italian movement disorder centers between 2016 and 2025. Turin (n = 151) was the primary cohort, while Rome (n = 49) provided supportive data for an exploratory comparison of early initiators. Early initiators started safinamide within 2 years of motor fluctuation onset; late initiators started treatment more than 2 years after onset. Treatment persistence, defined as time to permanent all-cause discontinuation, was analyzed using Kaplan-Meier methods and multivariable Cox models adjusted for age, Hoehn and Yahr stage, levodopa equivalent daily dose, and disease duration at motor fluctuation onset.

RESULTS: In Turin, 95 patients were early and 56 late initiators, with 71 discontinuations. Median persistence was 94 versus 34 months (log-rank p = 0.004). Early-initiator status remained associated with a lower discontinuation hazard after adjustment (HR 0.348, 95% CI 0.198-0.611; p < 0.001) and after excluding patients receiving device-aided therapies (HR 0.302, 95% CI 0.154-0.591; p < 0.001). Each additional year between fluctuation onset and safinamide initiation was associated with a higher discontinuation hazard (HR 1.104, 95% CI 1.034-1.179; p = 0.003). Among early initiators, persistence did not differ between centers, although follow-up was shorter in Rome.

CONCLUSION: Earlier safinamide initiation was associated with longer treatment persistence. This association may partly reflect differences in disease and treatment stage rather than timing itself, and prospective confirmation is needed.
