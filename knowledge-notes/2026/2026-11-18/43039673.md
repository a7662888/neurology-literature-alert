## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND: The α-synuclein origin model proposes brain-first and body-first Parkinson's disease (PD) subtypes, with speech potentially revealing their distinct neurodegenerative patterns.

OBJECTIVE: We aimed to analyze the possible speech and language differences between untreated PD patients with premotor (putative body-first) and without (putative brain-first) rapid eye movement sleep behavior disorder.

METHODS: Speech samples of reading passages and monologues were automatically analyzed using acoustic and linguistic features.

RESULTS: We enrolled 23 body-first and 35 brain-first de novo PD patients compared with 35 healthy controls. Brain-first patients showed greater speech impairment severity compared with body-first patients (P = 0.002) and controls (P < 0.001), whereas body-first patients showed greater language impairment severity compared with brain-first patients (P < 0.001) and controls (P < 0.001).

CONCLUSIONS: Putative brain-first subtype had more motor speech abnormalities whereas body-first subtype manifested greater language impairment. Thes finding is consistent with motor-dominant nigrostriatal involvement in brain-first and broader non-motor/cognitive involvement in body-first PD. © 2026 The Author(s).
