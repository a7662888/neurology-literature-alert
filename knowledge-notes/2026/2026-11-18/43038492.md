## 研究意義（Research Relevance）

本研究透過全基因體定序系統性篩檢453個基因，觀察到ALS基因譜與其他成人型神經退化性疾病基因存在重疊，此發現有助於擴展對ALS遺傳異質性之理解，並為後續以更大規模、具對照組之研究設計提供方向，以進一步釐清這些變異與ALS表現型之關聯強度。

## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Emerging evidence indicates that amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) shares genetic architecture with other neurologic disorders, whose causal variants may be overlooked by targeted sequencing. Here, we evaluated the occurrence of pathogenic variants in genes linked to adult-onset neurologic disorders among individuals with ALS.

METHODS: Short-read WGS was performed in retrospectively collected ALS (Gold Coast criteria) and primary lateral sclerosis (PLS) (consensus diagnostic criteria) patients consecutively recruited at an Italian tertiary referral center (2013-2023), extensively phenotyped to exclude mimics. Pathogenic or likely pathogenic single-nucleotide variants/indels (American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics guidelines) and short tandem repeat expansions across 453 genes, including established ALS genes and other neurodegenerative disease genes, were systematically screened and validated. C9orf72 expansions (>30 repeats) were routinely screened by fragment analysis and repeat-primed PCR.

RESULTS: A total of 856 patients with ALS and PLS (median age at onset 61.8 years, 38.5% women) were enrolled. Among them, 168 (19.6%) carried pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants or short tandem repeat expansions in genes associated with adult-onset neurologic disorders, mostly autosomal dominant. Of these, 41 (4.8% of the cohort) harbored variants in non-ALS/frontotemporal dementia (FTD) genes (ataxias, neuropathies, myopathies, movement disorders, mitochondrial diseases, and hereditary spastic paraplegia [HSP]). Repeat expansions accounted for 11.9% of cases, while 0.6% carried both sequence variants and repeat expansions. Family history of ALS/FTD (p = 2.132e-06), other neurodegenerative disorders (p = 0.0006934), and earlier age at onset (p = 0.024) were enriched among variant carriers. Variants in genes associated with upper motor neuron (UMN) involvement (ataxias/HSP) did not predict an UMN-predominant phenotype (odds ratio 1.54, 95% CI 0.39-6.07, p = 0.54), while the association between neuropathy/myopathy genes and a lower motor neuron-predominant phenotype was not statistically estimable. Beyond established ALS pathways, enrichment was observed in mitochondrial, metabolic, and proteostasis pathways (false discovery rate ≤0.05).

DISCUSSION: Although the absence of a control cohort limits assessment of variant enrichment, the identification of pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants in genes associated with adult-onset neurologic disorders other than ALS/FTD in patients with ALS supports the inclusion of broader gene panels to advance precision medicine in ALS.
