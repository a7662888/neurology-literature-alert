## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND: Neurological diseases, including stroke, Parkinson's disease (PD), and gliomas, have traditionally been interpreted through a focal-based lens, where symptoms are primarily attributed to the location and extent of neural damage. Converging evidence has reframed these conditions as circuitopathies, in which the disruption of brain networks underlies clinical manifestations. Yet, a network-integrated approach remains far from routine clinical practice. The aggregation of large datasets, which enables testing hypotheses about diagnosis, prognosis, or response to therapy across independent cohorts as well as prediction at the level of individual patients, is a critical step toward closing this translational gap.

METHODS: We describe the Clinical Connectome initiative developed within Work Package 2 of the EBRAINS 2.0 project, a translational effort that aims to bridge network neuroscience and clinical neurology. We first review stroke, PD, and gliomas as complementary models of brain network disruption, highlighting how the different pathophysiological mechanisms converge on shared patterns of network perturbation. We then present the Platform for Human Imaging, a GDPR-compliant infrastructure that enables secure storage, harmonized processing, and controlled dissemination of multimodal neuroimaging and clinical data across European institutions.

RESULTS: To date, the Clinical Connectome has aggregated data from over 7000 patients across institutions. A Delphi consensus panel, conducted in collaboration with the European Academy of Neurology (EAN), has established harmonized protocols for the prospective data acquisition in these disorders.

CONCLUSIONS: By providing an integrated framework linking clinical neuroimaging to brain atlases and computational modeling, the Clinical Connectome aims to lay the foundations for network-based precision neurology.
