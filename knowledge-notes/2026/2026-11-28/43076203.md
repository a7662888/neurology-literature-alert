## PubMed Abstract

BACKGROUND: Bradykinesia is central to diagnosing and monitoring Parkinson's disease (PD). Its severity is measured with ordinal clinical scales that require an expert, compress distinct motor features into one number, and show ceiling and floor effects.

OBJECTIVES: To develop and validate the Digital Bradykinesia Composite Score (DBCS), a measure of upper-limb bradykinesia derived from ordinary finger-tapping videos.

METHODS: We analyzed 3020 finger tapping videos from 656 participants (379 PD, 155 atypical parkinsonism, 33 rapid eye movement [REM] sleep behavior disorder, 79 controls, 10 other diagnoses) recorded at more than 20 sites. Computer vision extracted kinematic features, which were combined into four domains (vigor, reflecting tapping speed and amplitude; timing; irregularity; sequence effect) and the DBCS. We evaluated the DBCS against clinician ratings, levodopa and deep brain stimulation (DBS), dopamine transporter (DaT) imaging, substantia nigra free water, and 24 weeks of unsupervised home recordings.

RESULTS: The DBCS increased with clinical severity. It improved with levodopa and with DBS, with responsiveness similar to that of the clinical rating. In patients with DBS, levodopa was associated with a further improvement in the DBCS but not in the clinical rating. Over 24 weeks of home recordings, the DBCS increased in patients and decreased slightly in controls. Higher scores were associated with lower striatal DaT binding and higher nigral free water.

CONCLUSIONS: A composite score derived from ordinary video was associated with bradykinesia severity, treatment response, 6-month change, and markers of nigrostriatal degeneration, which supports its further evaluation for remote monitoring and as an endpoint in clinical trials. © 2026 International Parkinson and Movement Disorder Society.
