## PubMed Abstract

BackgroundDementia is the leading cause of death among women in Australia. Sex-specific dementia risk prediction models are rare for women at highest risk (≥70 years), limiting opportunities for targeted prevention.ObjectiveTo develop and externally validate the Senior women's Healthy brain Ageing: Risk Prediction-Western Australia (SHARP-WA), a prognostic model, for community-dwelling older women.MethodsModel development used 1358 women ≥ 70 years old without dementia at baseline from the Perth Longitudinal Study of Ageing Women (mean follow-up 12.2 years; 237 incident dementia events). External validation used 761 age-matched women from the English Longitudinal Study of Ageing (ELSA; mean follow-up 10.2 years; 94 incident dementia events). Predictors were selected using LASSO-penalized Cox proportional hazards regression and coefficients shrinkage-adjusted by bootstrap internal validation.ResultsThe SHARP-WA model included age, education, marital status, smoking, body mass index, hand grip strength, self-reported physical activity, diabetes, and hypertension. Internal discrimination was moderate across 3, 5, and 10-year prediction horizons (time-dependent C-statistic 0.65-0.67). SHARP-WA achieved higher discrimination than an age-only model (AUC 0.68 versus 0.62 at 10 years), confirming added value from modifiable risk factors. Calibration was good at all three horizons with minimal bias. External validation in ELSA showed 10-year discrimination of 0.62 (overall C-statistic 0.60, 95% CI 0.54-0.66); recalibration-in-the-large aligned predicted and observed risk (9.0% versus 8.0%). Decision curve analysis demonstrated positive net benefit across clinically relevant thresholds.ConclusionsSHARP-WA offers a pragmatic approach for estimating dementia risk in older women, moving beyond age-based stratification to incorporate demographic, lifestyle, and clinical risk factors relevant to dementia prevention.
